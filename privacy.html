Privacy Policy

Effective November 25, 2026

Sendstone lets you use the AI tools on your own computer, like Claude Code, Codex and Gemini, from your iPhone, iPad, Mac or Windows PC. It is built so that what you do with it stays with you. In this policy, "we" means the developer of Sendstone, and "your computer" means a computer you have installed Sendstone on.

The short version

  • We collect no data about you. There are no Sendstone accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking.
  • Your chats, your files and your AI sign-ins stay on your own devices.
  • Between the Sendstone apps (iPhone, iPad, Mac and Windows) and your computer, everything is encrypted end to end. When it goes through our relay, the relay cannot read it and stores nothing.
  • What you send to an AI goes to the company that runs that AI, under that company's own privacy policy.

What we collect

Nothing. Sendstone has no accounts, so there is no name, email address or password for us to keep. The apps contain no analytics, advertising or tracking code, and no outside code that collects data. We do not keep, sell or share any information about you.

If you email us, we receive your message and your email address, and use them only to answer you.

Where your things are kept

  • Chats and files. Your chats are kept on your computer, next to the AI tools and files they use, and shown on the devices you have paired with it. Chats you have straight from your phone, with your own API key or with a model that runs on the phone, are kept on that phone.
  • AI sign-ins. Each AI tool keeps its own sign-in on your computer. If you sign in to one from your phone, the AI's own sign-in runs on your computer and its page opens on your phone, so you sign in with the company itself. Sendstone never sees your password.
  • Pairing keys. When you pair a device, it and your computer make and exchange keys. Each device keeps its own key in its keychain, and your computer keeps its own. These keys stay on your devices. We never see them.
  • API keys. If you add an API key, it is kept in the keychain, or other protected storage, of the device you added it on. It is sent only to the AI service it belongs to.

What leaves your devices

Between your devices

The iPhone, iPad, Mac and Windows apps talk to your computer over a connection that is encrypted end to end: what one sends is sealed, and only the other can open it. At home they talk directly, over your own network. Away from home they connect through our relay, a small server on the internet that passes the sealed traffic between them.

The relay holds no keys, so it cannot read or change what it carries, and it stores nothing: no accounts, no messages, no history. Like any server, it sees which computer the traffic is for and the network address it comes from, and uses them only to pass the traffic on and to stop anyone from overloading it.

When the Mac or Windows app runs on the same computer as Sendstone, the two talk inside that computer, and nothing leaves it.

To the AI services you choose

When you use an AI, what you send it goes to the company that runs it, just as it does when you use that AI on your computer without Sendstone. The AI tools on your computer send your requests to their makers (for example, Claude Code to Anthropic, Codex to OpenAI and Gemini to Google). Chats that use your own API key go from your device straight to that service.

Those companies' own privacy policies and terms cover what they receive, so check them for the services you use. We are not part of that exchange and see none of it. A model that runs on your phone sends nothing anywhere.

Downloading a model to your phone

If you download a model to run on your phone, it comes straight from Hugging Face, the public library where it is published. Like any website, Hugging Face sees the request for the download.

Notifications

Notifications are optional. On an iPhone or iPad, your computer can let you know when a chat needs you or has finished. It sends the notification through our relay to Apple's push notification service, which delivers it to your device.

By default a notification says only what kind of thing happened, such as "A chat needs your approval". It includes more, such as the chat's name, only if you turn that on. The relay sees your device's notification address and the notification for the moment it takes to pass them on, and stores neither. Apple's own privacy policy covers its push service.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera, to scan the pairing code shown on your computer.
  • Local network, to find your computer on your Wi-Fi so the two can talk directly.
  • Photos, to attach pictures you choose to a chat, and to save an image from a chat when you ask.
  • Notifications, as described above.

You can change any of these at any time in your device's Settings.

This website

This website has no cookies, no analytics and no trackers, and loads nothing from anywhere else. It is hosted by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives your browser's network address when you visit, and handles it under GitHub's own privacy statement.

Children

Sendstone is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone.

Your choices

Because we keep nothing about you, there is nothing for us to delete. Everything is on your own devices, and you are in control of it:

  • Remove a paired device in the app's Settings, under Devices that can use this computer. It is cut off at once.
  • Remove an API key in the app at any time.
  • Turn notifications off in your device's Settings.
  • When you uninstall Sendstone from a computer, your chats are kept in case you come back. You can choose to delete them as well.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
